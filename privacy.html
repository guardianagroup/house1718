PRIVACY POLICY · 19 NOVEMBER 2026

No account, no cookies, no selling data.

Elixire does not ask for your name, phone number or email, uses no cookies or analytics, and does not sell or share data. Even so, it needs servers so that people can find and hear each other. This is what each of them sees.

1. This website

The website is served from GitHub Pages, by GitHub, Inc. (United States). Like any web server, GitHub receives your IP address, your browser and the page you request; what it keeps is explained in its privacy statement. The pages load nothing from other companies: typefaces, images and libraries are served from this same site.

2. Cookies and storage

Elixire does not use cookies. In your browser’s storage it keeps only the language you choose and, if needed, a technical connection preference. While you are sharing an invitation, its secret is kept in that tab and erased when you join the conversation or close it. None of this is sent to anyone. To erase it all, clear the site data for house1718.com in your browser settings.

3. The conversation

Messages, voice notes, photos and files are encrypted in your browser, end to end. The GUARDIANA server that carries them (the relay, in Nuremberg, Germany, or in Miami, United States) cannot read them: it sees your IP address, the room (an anonymous identifier), the times and the amount of data. It keeps no names or keys, and its code writes no logs: what it needs is held in memory while the conversation lasts. If your network blocks the connection, it may go through another GUARDIANA server that only forwards the same encrypted data.

The invitation: with the link, the conversation’s secret travels in the part of the link that the browser never sends to any server. If a code is used instead, the secret waits on the relay, locked with a key derived from that code, for at most 10 minutes and for a single use.

When the conversation ends, what was left of it in the browsers is erased: messages, files and keys.

4. Calls and video calls

They always go through GUARDIANA’s call servers, in Nuremberg (Germany) or Miami (United States). These see the IP addresses of the people talking, the times and the volume, not the picture or the sound, and are configured without logs. Picture and sound are end-to-end encrypted and also carry a post-quantum layer that only the browsers in the conversation can open. The camera and microphone are only used during the call and with your permission.

5. “Check”

The analysis of the face, expressions and voice runs entirely on your device, on the picture and sound you already receive: nothing is sent or stored, and the other person sees nothing. The only thing asked of their browser, over the encrypted channel, is the type of camera they use (phone, physical or virtual and, if it is a known virtual camera program, which one), never the device name.

6. What the other person receives

What you send, your picture and voice during the call and, if they press “Check”, the type of your camera.

7. What we do not do

We do not sell or share data. There is no advertising, no analytics, no accounts and no profiles of the people who use Elixire. Nothing is charged today, so we process no payment data.

8. If you write to us

If you write to hola@guardianagroup.com, we keep that email to answer you and for nothing else. It is deleted one year after the last reply, or earlier if you ask.

9. Your rights, legal basis and complaints

You can ask us what data of yours we hold, to correct it or to delete it, by writing to hola@guardianagroup.com. Almost everything on this page lives only in your browser or in the memory of a conversation that has already ended, so it does not exist anywhere we could look for it. We process connection data because it is needed to provide the service you ask for, and your email, if you write to us, with your consent, which you can withdraw at any time. Elixire’s servers are in Germany and the United States; GitHub, which serves the website, in the United States. If you think we handle your data wrongly, you can complain to the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio (Colombia), the AEPD (Spain) or the data protection authority of your country; first, write to us and we will sort it out.
